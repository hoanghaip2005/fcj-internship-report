## Sharing and feedback on the program

## 1. Reflections on the FCAJ internship

First Cloud AI Journey introduced cloud computing through a learning path connected to technical writing, community events and practical products. As an Information Technology student, connecting programming, networking and databases within an AWS architecture made my learning objectives clearer.

Preparing a twelve-week worklog, five blogs, four event reflections and FLASHRUSH materials developed both research and communication skills. These comments describe my learning and report-writing experience. Suggestions express personal preferences for improvement rather than establishing that the program lacks those activities.

### Satisfaction level

I am **satisfied** with First Cloud AI Journey. The AWS learning path, community activities and learning-product requirements helped me identify a direction, develop independent research and explain technical concepts. Integrating services in FLASHRUSH provided a more concrete view of developing and operating cloud applications.

I would value additional practical review milestones, clear verification criteria and opportunities to discuss real errors. Personally, I need to keep updating the journal, preparing questions and saving evidence after practice to benefit more from learning opportunities.

## 2. What I found valuable

### 2.1. Connecting foundations to applications

IAM, compute, storage, databases and networking provide a basis for understanding architecture rather than isolated services. Analyzing DropIt, FitJoy and FLASHRUSH helped me ask questions about data flows, access, scaling and cost.

An effective approach is to choose a small requirement, investigate suitable services, practice and explain the choices. This also identifies gaps for further reading or discussion.

### 2.2. Blogs and event reflections

Blog writing required organizing knowledge for other readers. Karpenter/EKS, S3 Express One Zone, Cognito, SQS/Lambda and DynamoDB idempotency offered different opportunities to define problems, explain workflows and state applicability conditions.

Event reflections broadened my view of Cloud, AI and data. I learned to distinguish the program's topics from my extended analysis and proposed applications, keeping ideas separate from completed outcomes.

### 2.3. Learning through a concrete product

FLASHRUSH connects several services in one business flow. Questions about HTTP 202 versus successful purchase, duplicate messages and DLQ/order-state relationships strengthened my distributed-systems understanding.

Documentation also showed that a practical product involves more than a working interface. Permissions, failure cases, test evidence, cost and cleanup require attention.

## 3. Suggestions for improving the learning experience

| Suggestion | Possible implementation | Expected value |
|---|---|---|
| Stage-level completion criteria | Checklists for knowledge, labs and submitted evidence | Help students track progress and completion conditions |
| Guidance for adapting report templates | Mark sample text and provide short examples and personal-information checks | Reduce leftover template content and inappropriate claims |
| Practical error discussions | Teams explain one error, its evidence and resolution | Develop diagnosis beyond following successful steps |
| Architecture review before deployment | Discuss data flow, IAM, cost and limitations | Identify missing configuration or overly broad goals early |
| Evidence-capture guidance | Agree screenshot names, context, configuration and sensitive-data redaction | Support comparison and handover |
| Document version management | Associate instructions with a source snapshot or commit | Avoid mismatches between source and guidance |
| Cost and cleanup checklist | Check Region, inventory, budget and log retention from the start | Build responsible lab-resource management habits |

### 3.1. Practical learning content

I would value labs covering the successful path and controlled failures such as missing IAM permissions, incorrect CORS, a Worker not receiving messages or orders remaining PENDING. Each case should include symptoms, log locations and recovery criteria instead of only the final configuration.

Multi-service exercises can use small checkpoints: validate the backend, integrate the API, then connect the frontend and monitoring. This helps learners locate the failing component.

### 3.2. Questions and feedback

I suggest a short technical-question format: objective, environment, current step, observed error, evidence and attempted solutions. Feedback can use shared criteria such as correctness, security, reproducibility and documentation quality.

My responsibility is to prepare clear questions, consult documentation before asking and update instructions after resolving an issue. This also makes better use of support time.

## 4. My contributions

The current report includes detailed documentation for twelve weeks, separating completed work from future plans, five technical blogs and four event reflections. The FLASHRUSH proposal and workshop describe a consistent Serverless application flow from architecture through configuration and testing guidance.

These are personal learning products. I do not present them as formally accepted program materials or officially adopted documentation. I want to improve accuracy, add practical evidence and share them after appropriate review.

Further contributions could include:

- Summarizing practice errors, investigation steps and fixes.
- Reviewing terms, links and article structure for new learners.
- Adding captioned evidence and distinguishing diagrams from real Console screenshots.
- Explaining source limitations and simulated functions.
- Sharing cost-monitoring and inventory practices after implementation and verification.

## 5. Expectations for the next stage

I would like more opportunities to present architecture, receive technical feedback and practice a product through configuration, testing, monitoring and cleanup. For FLASHRUSH, the next priority is completing manual-flow evidence in an authorized environment before expanding functionality beyond verified foundations.

### Would I recommend the program to friends?

**Yes.** I would recommend it to friends seeking a structured AWS learning direction who are willing to study independently, practice and write documentation. I would also explain that outcomes depend on active learning, verification and openness to feedback. Reading instructions or polishing a report alone is not enough to master the technical work.

## 6. Acknowledgements

I thank the organizers, instructors and First Cloud AI Journey community for access to the AWS learning path and knowledge-sharing activities. I welcome further feedback to improve my technical knowledge, learning products and working practices.
